# Sonic Stage adds spatial action cues when dialogue blocks audio description

> Sonic Stage turns dialogue-heavy video into an interactive spatial soundscape for blind and low-vision viewers. A 12-person study found that the added cues improved comprehension, spatial presence, and engagement.

Conventional audio description usually waits for gaps in speech, so important actions can disappear from a busy conversation. Sonic Stage instead spatializes each voice, generates sounds tied to visible actions, and lets a viewer request a description for extra context.

The system first reconstructs a stable scene across camera cuts, then maps people and actions into headphone audio. This allows movement and layout to remain audible without placing another continuous narrator over the dialogue.

The evaluation covered 12 blind or low-vision viewers and selected clips. Generated sounds or descriptions can still be wrong, and the paper does not report a production deployment across full films or varied genres.

## Why this was selected

It treats accessibility as an audiovisual design problem and uses space, interaction, and sound effects to carry information that narration cannot fit.

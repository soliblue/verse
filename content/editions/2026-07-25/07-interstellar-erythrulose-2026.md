# The first sugar found in interstellar space was not the simplest one

> Radio spectra from a Galactic-centre cloud match erythrulose, a four-carbon sugar. The smaller three-carbon sugars expected to be easier to make were not detected and appear at least 8 to 17 times less abundant.

The identification combines 17 rotational transitions in the molecular cloud G+0.693. Six of the cleanest lines give a reported chance-alignment probability of 0.2 percent, although line blending remains a concern in such a chemically crowded spectrum.

Erythrulose can be assembled in ice models from two smaller two-carbon fragments that are already abundant in the cloud. The authors’ models overproduce the missing three-carbon sugars, so the chemical pathway is not yet settled.

The result does not show that interstellar sugar reached early Earth, and detecting a chiral molecule does not reveal which handed form is present. It does show that molecular size alone is a poor guide to which prebiotic compounds can accumulate in space.

## Why this was selected

The surprising part is not only finding a sugar between the stars, but finding the larger member before the supposedly simpler ones.

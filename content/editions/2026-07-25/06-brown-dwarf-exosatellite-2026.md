# A Jupiter-mass object may be orbiting a brown dwarf

> Astronomers found a roughly 170-day wobble in the light of a directly imaged brown dwarf. The best fit is an orbiting companion with a minimum mass near 0.9 Jupiters, large enough to blur the boundary between moon, planet, and binary partner.

The team applied radial-velocity analysis to spectra of CD-35 2722 B from the VLT’s CRIRES+ instrument. The method looks for periodic shifts in spectral lines as an unseen body pulls its host towards and away from us.

The authors describe this as the first time Doppler monitoring has produced evidence for a satellite around a companion brown dwarf. It is evidence rather than a settled detection, and the model includes additional inner signals that are less secure.

Even the noun is unresolved. There is no agreed criterion that makes an object this massive an exomoon rather than another kind of substellar companion.

## Why this was selected

The same measurement that reveals the object also breaks an intuitive category: a satellite can be planetary in mass without clearly being a planet.

# Catfish melanoma is the first transmissible cancer documented in fish

> Melanomas in Lake Memphremagog catfish are not separate cancers caused by the same pollution or pathogen. Genome sequencing shows that the cancer cells themselves form one infectious lineage moving between fish.

Raised black tumours affected 23 to 37% of sampled brown bullheads between 2014 and 2017. Researchers compared each tumour with normal tissue from its host and with tumours from other fish.

The tumour genomes were more closely related to one another than to the fish carrying them. Hundreds of thousands of shared variants appeared in the tumours but not their hosts, which is the signature expected when living cancer cells pass between animals.

This is the first documented transmissible cancer in fish and only the fourth animal group known to harbour one. Its origin, method of transmission, geographic range, and long-term effect on the population remain unknown.

## Why this was selected

One clean genetic comparison overturns the intuitive model of cancer as a disease that always begins inside each individual.

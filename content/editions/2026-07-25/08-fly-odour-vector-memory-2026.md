# Fruit flies remember the direction back to a lost odour plume

> Fruit flies do more than react when a smell disappears. In a controlled virtual odour world, they stored a direction back to the plume’s edge and updated that internal compass as they moved.

Head-fixed flies walked on an air-supported ball while a moving nozzle controlled wind direction and odour concentration. This let the researchers create precise chemical landscapes that would be impossible to see or stabilize in open air.

The flies tracked an attractive plume by repeatedly leaving it and making directed returns to its boundary. Behavioural models, calcium imaging, and neural perturbations pointed to FC2 neurons in the fan-shaped body as the signal for the direction back.

The memory appeared to encode direction, not distance. The experiment shows a reusable navigation strategy in tethered flies, but whether freely moving animals use the same calculation in a turbulent natural plume remains unresolved.

# METR puts autonomous AI optimization’s useful budget below $3,000

> METR compared what humans and AI agents achieve for the same money on a real model-training optimization problem. Current agents helped at small budgets, but their advantage ended somewhere between zero and $3,000.

METR calls the crossover an “expenditure horizon”. Instead of asking whether an agent beats one benchmark score, it plots improvement against the full cost of model calls, experiments, and human work.

For the NanoGPT speedrun, interviews and code analysis suggested that a one-per-cent training-time improvement costs roughly 16 hours or $2,500 of human labour. Six agent runs spent more than $10,000 in total, yet their estimated expenditure horizons remained between zero and $3,000.

Revalidation erased apparent gains from GPT-5 and Opus 4.1, while GPT-5.5 and Opus 4.8 made significant but modest improvements. The result covers one small training task, excludes human-AI teams, and may be affected by an inefficient harness or undisclosed training exposure.

## Why this was selected

It replaces a vague claim that AI accelerates AI research with a cost curve, then shows exactly where the current evidence stops supporting that claim.

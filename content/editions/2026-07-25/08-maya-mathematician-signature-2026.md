# A Maya calculation ends with its author’s name

> A reconstructed wall text at Xultun compresses six calendrical and astronomical cycles into 2,920 days, then closes with “so says Sak Tahn Waax.” It is the only known direct personal credit for mathematical work in the Classic Maya corpus.

The key number is the least common multiple of a 584-day Venus cycle and a 365-day solar year: five Venus cycles equal eight solar years. The text nests shorter intervals involving 20-day, 260-day, 360-day, Mars, solar, and Venus counts inside that span.

The calculation survives in nine mathematical glyphs, followed by two glyphs of attribution. The paper translates the name as “White-chested Fox” and dates the best-fitting starting point to 781 CE.

Parts of the inscription are eroded, so dates and omitted shorthand have been reconstructed from the visible sequence and other Maya conventions. “Mathematician-astronomer” is also a modern description, not a recorded Classic Maya title.

## Why this was selected

The arithmetic is compact enough to follow, but the strongest consequence is personal: an ancient calculation stops being anonymous.

# AI-redesigned enzymes reach 79-fold higher target specificity after evolution

> Researchers used an AI model to stabilize enzymes before evolving them in the laboratory. The stronger starting points reached useful protein variants that the natural enzymes could not, including one with more than 79 times greater target specificity.

ProteinMPNN redesigned the sequences of three botulinum neurotoxin proteases while preserving their catalytic function and improving stability. The researchers then evolved the redesigned and natural versions side by side using phages that repeatedly mutate and select proteins.

Across four campaigns, the redesigned enzymes adapted faster and reached mutations that failed in the natural protein backgrounds. One redesigned protease evolved to cut ataxin-2 while minimizing its original target, producing more than 79-fold greater selected specificity than the best variant evolved from the natural enzyme.

The work shows a way to give laboratory evolution more room to explore, not a finished therapy. The experiments covered three related enzymes, and every useful design still required physical selection and validation.

## Why this was selected

The AI did not guess the final answer. It changed the starting landscape so ordinary evolution could reach answers that were previously inaccessible.

# EU AI labels become mandatory on 2 August

> The European Commission has clarified the AI Act transparency rules that start on 2 August 2026. Products must disclose direct AI interaction, generators must embed machine-readable marks, and professional publishers face separate visible-label duties.

Providers of chatbots, agents, and avatars must tell people from the first interaction that they are dealing with AI, unless that is already obvious. Providers of generative systems must make synthetic audio, images, video, and text detectable through effective machine-readable marks.

Deployers have a different job. They must visibly or audibly disclose deepfakes and label AI-generated public-interest text unless it received substantive human review and editorial control; spell-checking alone does not qualify.

Source code, machine-to-machine output, closed-loop development material, and standard editing can fall outside the marking duty. Enforcement is split among national authorities and EU bodies, so teams that do not follow the voluntary code may have to prove equivalent compliance case by case.

## Why this was selected

It gives European product and media teams an immediate checklist and separates three duties that are easy to confuse: interface disclosure, embedded provenance, and visible publication labels.
